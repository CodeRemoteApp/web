Privacy Policy

Effective October 26, 2026

In short: Code Remote doesn't collect, store or sell any personal data. It has no accounts, no analytics, no ads and no servers of its own. Your phone connects to your own computer, and what you see and type stays between the two.

This policy covers the Code Remote iPhone app (the "App") and the coderemote command-line tool for your computer (the "CLI"), together "Code Remote". Code Remote is made by Thanh Tran ("we", "us").

What we collect

Nothing. We don't operate any server that the App or the CLI reports to. We don't use analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking SDKs, and we don't track you across other apps or websites. We have no way to see your files, your git repositories, your terminal sessions or the commands you run.

What stays on your devices

On your iPhone

  • Pairings: the address of each computer you pair with and its access token, stored in the iOS Keychain.
  • Tunnel sign-in tokens: the token Microsoft Dev Tunnels issues after you sign in, stored in the iOS Keychain.
  • Settings: your preferences in the App, stored on the device.

Deleting a computer in the App, or deleting the App, removes this data from your iPhone. It may be included in your own encrypted device backups, under Apple's control.

On your computer

The CLI keeps its access token and configuration in ~/.coderemote/ (readable only by your user account). Running coderemote rotate-token revokes every paired device.

Device permissions

  • Camera: used only to scan the pairing QR code that the CLI shows. Images are processed on the device and never saved or sent anywhere.
  • Face ID: optional, to unlock computers you choose to lock. Face ID is handled entirely by iOS; the App only learns whether authentication succeeded.

Third-party services

To reach your computer from anywhere, Code Remote uses services run by other companies. They process data under their own privacy policies, not ours:

  • Microsoft Dev Tunnels. Traffic between the App and your computer passes through Microsoft's Dev Tunnels relay, encrypted in transit (HTTPS / WSS). The CLI downloads Microsoft's Dev Tunnels command-line tool on first run, and that tool may send diagnostic data to Microsoft. See the Microsoft Privacy Statement.
  • GitHub or Microsoft sign-in. When you sign in to Dev Tunnels, you do so on GitHub's or Microsoft's own page in the system browser. We never see your password. See the GitHub Privacy Statement.
  • npm. Installing the CLI downloads it from the npm registry, operated by GitHub.
  • Apple. If you download the App from the App Store, Apple may share anonymous usage and crash statistics with us when you've agreed to that in iOS settings. These are aggregated by Apple and don't identify you.

Commands you run in the terminal, such as git push or a package install, connect wherever those programs connect, exactly as if you ran them at your computer.

Children

Code Remote is a developer tool and isn't directed at children under 13. Because we collect no data, we don't knowingly collect data from children.

Your rights

Because we hold no personal data about you, there is nothing for us to access, correct, export or delete. You control everything Code Remote stores, on your own devices, as described above.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update it on this page and change the effective date above. If a change ever means Code Remote starts collecting data, we'll say so in the App before it happens.
